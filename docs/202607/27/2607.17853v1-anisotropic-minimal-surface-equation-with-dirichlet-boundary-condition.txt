ANISOTROPIC MINIMAL SURFACE EQUATION WITH DIRICHLET BOUNDARY CONDITION 

LU CHEN, JIALI LAN, AND HAOLIN LIU 

Abstract. This paper investigates the Dirichlet problem for the anisotropic minimal sur-face equation in a bounded domain. Under the natural assumption of non-negative boundary anisotropic mean curvature, we establish the unique solvability of the Dirichlet problem for continuous boundary data. To achieve this, an essential a priori gradient estimate is estab-lished, which also allows us to prove a weak version of Bernstein’s theorem for entire solutions with linear growth. Moreover, using the direct method in the calculus of variations, we prove the existence and local Lipschitz regularity of generalized minimizers in BV (Ω) with L1(∂Ω) boundary data. We also find that this variational formulation naturally yields a Neumann boundary condition, geometrically explaining why the Neumann problem requires no boundary curvature constraints.

The solvability of its Dirichlet problem has a celebrated history. For n = 2, results of Bernstein [6], Haar [25], Rad´ o [34], Finn [20], and Finn-Osserman [22] show that the Dirichlet problem is well-posed for arbitrary continuous boundary data if and only if Ω is convex. Recently, Cui-Yip [10] considered anisotropic graphical mean curvature flow with Dirichlet boundary datum in arbitrary dimensions (see their Section 4 for details). Their approach yields boundary gradient estimates under a weighted mean-convexity condition involving the constants 

γ1, γ 2, which depend on quantitative assumptions measuring the deviation of the anisotropic integrand F from the isotropic integrand and on the tangential C2-size of the Dirichlet boundary datum. More specifically, their assumptions require F to be close to the isotropic function and the Dirichlet boundary datum not to change too quickly along ∂Ω. They further discussed the associated elliptic translator problem and the long-time behavior of the flow. In the present paper we focus on the stationary zero-speed case, namely the Dirichlet problem for the anisotropic minimal surface equation. Our result recovers and refines the correspond-ing zero-speed Dirichlet case, the main difference lies in the boundary hypothesis. Instead of imposing a weighted curvature condition, we assume the natural anisotropic mean-convexity of the vertical cylinder ∂Ω × R is non-negative in the sense of Wang-Xia [41]. Hence, in the common zero-speed Dirichlet setting, our result provides a more intrinsic geomet-ric solvability criterion and removes the smallness requirements on the boundary data and on the deviation of F from the isotropic case.
