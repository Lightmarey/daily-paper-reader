Abstract. We construct an incidence-driven tropical approximation of the planar Aleksandrov Monge–Ampère equation. Let Ω ⊂ R2 be a bounded open convex domain, let K ⋐ Ω, and let 

FN = GPN 0Ω be the minimal nonnegative concave tropical series with integral slopes, zero boundary values, and corner locus containing an N -point set PN ⊂ K. For universally generic configurations whose empirical measures converge to μ,

N −1/2FN −→ Fμ, Ω

uniformly on Ω, where Fμ, Ω is the unique continuous concave Aleksandrov solution of MA( F ) = μ

with zero boundary values. On every compact L ⋐ Ω we prove an O(N −1/2) bounded-Lipschitz-type estimate for the curvature discrepancy. No regularity or strict-convexity assumption is imposed on 

∂Ω.For rational polygons, strong genericity holds on an open dense full-measure locus. The tropical curve has exactly N bounded cells, the marked dual edges form a spanning tree, and every compact internal edge has weight one. These finite statements yield global weak curvature convergence and the exact identity

We construct an incidence-driven integral-affine approximation of the planar Aleksandrov Monge– Ampère equation. Let Ω ⊂ R2 be bounded and convex, and let P ⊂ Ω be finite. Among all nonnegative concave tropical series with integral slopes, zero boundary values, and corner locus containing P , there is a pointwise-minimal element 

FΩ,P = GP 0Ω.

If |P | = N , tropical interpolation forces the natural height scale √N . We prove that, when the empirical measures of the marked points converge, N −1/2FΩ,P converges to the zero-boundary Aleksandrov solution of the corresponding Monge–Ampère equation. The determinant operator is not built into the discrete model. The relaxation is selected only by incidence and minimality. Its Monge–Ampère measure is recovered from the topology of the tropical curve and the lattice areas of dual Newton polygons: Euler characteristic and Pick’s formula give an exact local source–curvature identity, minimality forces primitive compact edges, and coarea converts the remaining topological error into an O(N −1/2) discrepancy of measures. The microscopic construction has exact GL (2 , Z) covariance; the limit has the full affine covariance of the Monge–Ampère equation. The result has two levels. On every bounded convex planar domain we obtain uniform convergence of the potentials and a quantitative curvature estimate on compact subsets. No boundary smoothness or strict convexity is required. On rational polygons the finite tropical geometry is globally controlled, giving open genericity, an explicit marked topology, global curvature convergence, and an exact boundary correction to the total Monge–Ampère mass. 1.1. 1.2. Relation to previous work. The construction developed here starts from an established finite-source theory. The limit studied in this paper is different from the earlier scaling limits. The category of Ω-tropical series, the wave operators Gp, the pointwise-minimal relaxation GP 0Ω, finite canonical presentations on rational polygons, and the symplectic-area comparison principle were developed in [KS18, Sections 5–6 and 14]. The sandpile theorem of [KS26, Propositions 4.8 and 5.4] keeps a finite source set P fixed and lets the lattice mesh tend to zero, identifying the corresponding Abelian-sandpile odometer with the tropical relaxation GP 0Ω; its mesh threshold may depend on the complete fixed configuration. The continuous tropical sandpile and its self-organized-criticality interpretation were introduced and studied in [Kal+18], following the earlier observation of lattice strings in [CPS10]. Tropical wavefronts and the instantaneous polygonization of positive-time cores for arbitrary convex domains were established in [MS23, Proposition 24 and Corollary 25]. That theory concerns the canonical unmarked tropical distance and its caustic; it does not address marked minimal relaxations or empirical-measure limits. The closest earlier finite-N topological result is [KP23, Theorem 3.3]. It proves, for generic finite configurations, that the bounded-face genus of GP 0Ω equals |P | and gives a tree statement after removing the marked points; the same paper reports numerical evidence that the tropical degree has order p|P | and that typical curves are close to a square-grid pattern. We do not claim the bare identity g = |P | as new, and none of the arguments below depends on the proof in that paper. What is new here is the semilinear open full-measure genericity theorem, the complete marked primal–dual topology, and the use of that topology as a quantitative curvature mechanism. None of these cited works proves a continuum limit in which the number of marked points tends to infinity and their empirical measures converge, and none identifies a nonlinear PDE governing that limit. The contributions of the present paper are the following. (i) Many-point Monge–Ampère limit. We let N = |PN | → ∞ in the tropical relaxation itself and prove that N −1/2GPN 0Ω converges to the zero-boundary Aleksandrov solution with Monge– Ampère measure equal to the weak limit of the empirical source measures. The theorem holds on every bounded convex planar domain for source clouds contained in one fixed K ⋐ Ω.(ii) Quantitative curvature convergence. On every compact interior set we prove an O(N −1/2)

bounded-Lipschitz-type estimate comparing the normalized atomic Monge–Ampère measure of the tropical roof with the empirical source measure. On rational polygons the estimate combines with exact total-mass control to give weak convergence on Ω.(iii) Deterministic square-root complexity and normalization. The earlier numerical observation of a √N degree scale is replaced by deterministic O(√N ) bounds obtained from tropical interpolation and transferred to the minimal relaxation by the symplectic-area engine. These bounds control weighted tropical length, ordinary length, and boundary quasi-degree without genericity. When the empirical measures have a nonzero weak limit, the continuum theorem confirms N −1/2 as the correct macroscopic normalization of the potentials. (iv) A strengthened finite topology. For rational polygons we construct a finite semilinear coefficient model and an open dense full-measure strongly generic locus. The marked dual edges form a spanning tree, cutting the marked carriers gives a primal tree, reidentification produces a finite core of first Betti number N , and every component outside that core is a boundary tree attached once. This refines the earlier genus/tree statement and makes the marked incidence structure usable in deformation arguments. (v) Curvature from topology and minimality. Euler characteristic and Pick’s formula give an exact local source–curvature identity. Coefficient-lowering and tree–cotree deformations force every compact internal edge to have weight one. The remaining discrepancy is localized at terminal 6 KALININ, LUPERCIO, SERRANO, AND SHKOLNIKOV 

branches, yielding both the quantitative estimate and the exact polygonal identity 

MA( GP 0Ω)(Ω ◦) = |P | − 1 + 12 Dterm (GP 0Ω).

(vi) Extension beyond polygons. An intrinsic countable genericity condition, exact rational-core reduction, and a weighted Crofton estimate extend the potential and compact-interior curvature limits to arbitrary bounded convex domains, including domains with corners, flat pieces, and irrational supporting directions. (vii) Affine and probabilistic consequences. We obtain almost-sure limits for i.i.d. source clouds and the full area-preserving affine covariance of the continuum solution. Returning to the discrete Abelian sandpile uses only the earlier fixed-source theorem and therefore yields a configuration-dependent diagonal corollary; no uniform simultaneous (N, h ) limit is claimed. Thus the new result is not a refinement of the fixed-source sandpile limit alone. It is a many-source law in which a fully nonlinear affine-covariant PDE emerges from incidence constraints, planar tropical topology, lattice area, and minimality. 1.3. Proof architecture. The proof has four interacting parts. Square-root interpolation. A Hahn-field dependence produces a tropical polynomial through the prescribed points, with gradients in a square of radius O(√N ) [Hah07; DSS05]. A tropical-distance barrier imposes zero boundary values. The symplectic-area engine of Theorem 3.6 then identifies the weighted edge length with the boundary quasi-degree sum and makes the minimal relaxation no more expensive than any zero-boundary tropical polynomial through the marks. The explicit interpolating competitor satisfies that admissible class, giving 

Asymp (V (FN )) + H1(V (FN )) + D∂ (FN ) = OΩ,K (√N ).

This deterministic estimate uses no genericity. Marked topology and local curvature. A finite slope bound makes the minimal coefficient vector semilinear and piecewise affine in the marked configuration. A dimension argument gives g(V (FN )) = 

N , where g is the number of bounded complementary cells, and identifies the marked dual edges as a spanning tree. Long carriers parallel to polygon sides create no additional modulus; multiple marks on one carrier form a positive-codimension discriminant. For a regular window U ⋐ Ω, Euler characteristic and Pick’s formula yield 

MA( FP )( U ) = #( P ∩ U ) + 12 BP (U ) − cP (U ) + Ξ P (U ).

Minimality, coarea, and Crofton control. Coefficient-lowering deformations eliminate interior lattice points from the relevant Newton polygons and force every compact internal edge to have weight one. Signed layer cake and one-dimensional coarea on the tropical graph then bound the curvature discrepancy by tangential variation of the test function. On arbitrary convex domains, the rational-core theorem gives a finite polygonal model at every positive level and identifies the direct relaxation there with a shifted zero-boundary polygonal relaxation. A common tropical-distance barrier controls the active gradients on fixed interior rectangles, and weighted Crofton estimates give the local 

O(√N ) length and excess bounds uniformly over the exhausting polygons. Compactness and identification. Aleksandrov’s maximum principle gives a common boundary modu-lus, concavity gives interior compactness, and stability of Monge–Ampère measure identifies every subsequential limit. Uniqueness of the zero-boundary Dirichlet problem removes subsequences. The exact terminal formula supplies the additional tightness needed for weak convergence on a closed rational polygon. Constancy of nonemptiness and dimension prevents appearances or disappearances; the coincidence and order predicates prevent unrecorded mergers; and the closure predicates prevent changes of incidence.
