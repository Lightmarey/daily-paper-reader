ON THE WELL-POSEDNESS OF POROUS MEDIUM EQUATIONS ON GENERAL METRIC MEASURE SPACES 

DIWEN CHANG Abstract . On general metric measure spaces, we develop a well-posedness theory for the Cauchy problem associated with the signed porous medium equation and its fast di ff usion counterpart 

∂tu = L(|u|m−1u), m > 0,

where L is the generator of a symmetric Dirichlet form. We prove that, for every initial datum 

u0 ∈ Lm+1(M, μ ), there exists a unique weak solution in the natural energy space associated with the Dirichlet form. The proof relies on the Rothe method and the theory of monotone operators, and only requires the Dirichlet form and its extended Dirichlet space; no additional regularity or geometric assumptions are imposed. Consequently, the theory applies to a wide range of metric measure spaces, including non-smooth fractals.

To the best of our knowledge, no systematic well-posedness theory for the PME or FDE has been established in this fractal setting. This case is pre-cisely a primary motivation for the present work. Consequently, equation (1.1) unifies both local and non-local quasilinear di ff usion processes on a wide variety of geometries, ranging from Euclidean domains and Riemannian manifolds to self-similar fractals. We begin by briefly recalling the relevant literature. In the classical Euclidean setting, both the PME and the FDE have been systematically treated in the monograph [54] and the references therein. Their fractional counterparts, the FPME and FFDE, were first introduced in the pioneering works [12, 13]. Beyond the Euclidean case, the PME and FDE have been extensively studied on Riemannian manifolds, where geometric properties such as curvature play a prominent role. On manifolds with non-positive sectional curvature, such as hyperbolic spaces and Cartan–Hadamard manifolds, well-posedness and asymptotic behaviour for broad classes of initial data have been addressed in a substantial body of work (see, e.g., [7, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 55]). The FPME on manifolds was recently studied via a potential method tailored to non-positively curved settings [6]. This strategy was later adapted to the PME on manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature by means of a Green function technique [29]. More recently, the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the Leibenson equation (which reduces to the PME or FDE when p = 2) was established on arbitrary geodesically complete Riemannian manifolds, without imposing any curvature restriction [52]. A natural question then arises: how can one define and study the PME on non-smooth metric measure spaces such as fractals? The main obstacle is that fractals lack a di ff erential structure, so the classical Laplace–Beltrami operator is not directly available. Nevertheless, an alternative framework has been developed. Analysis on fractals originated with the probabilistic construction of Brownian motion on the Sierpi´ nski gasket [4] and the Sierpi´ nski carpet [2]. Subsequently, based on graph approximations, an analytic framework for defining a Laplacian on a wide class ON THE WELL-POSEDNESS OF PMES ON GENERAL METRIC MEASURE SPACES 3

of fractals was developed [38]. All these constructions—whether probabilistic or analytic—are unified within the theory of Dirichlet forms [10, 21]. In this framework, a given regular Dirichlet form is associated with a unique non-positive definite self-adjoint operator, which serves precisely as the Laplacian on the fractal [10, 21]. Over the past four decades, significant progress has been made in the study of PDEs on fractals and general metric measure spaces, ranging from the heat equation and heat kernel estimates [3, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 41] to the wave equation [11, 51], semilinear PDEs [16, 17, 18, 19], and vector analysis /first-order PDEs [35, 36, 37]. These works have profoundly revealed how fractal geometry, in particular fractal dimensions, a ff ects the behaviour of solutions. Nevertheless, research on equation (1.1) on general metric measure spaces remains limited. The works [46, 47] established a well-posedness theory for the stochastic version of the more general equation ∂tu = L(P(u)) (which covers the power case P(u) = |u|m−1u) on general measure spaces. Their framework, however, requires that the underlying Dirichlet form be transient and relies essentially on stochastic analysis tools such as the Lp-Itˆ o formula. While natural in the stochastic context, these tools impose additional regularity on the solution that is not required in the purely deterministic setting. On the deterministic side, the closest result we are aware of is in [1, Section 3], which also establishes a well-posedness theory for ∂tu = L(P(u)). However, their framework is not tailored to the power case P(u) = |u|m−1u. Indeed, [1, Theorem 3.4] requires that P′ take values in a closed subinterval of (0 , ∞), a condition which clearly fails for the power function. Alternatively, although [1, Theorem 3.7] can be applied to this power case, it does so under the additional assumptions of finite total measure and L∞ initial data. More fundamentally, their framework is built upon a Gelfand triple, which naturally enforces the condition P(u) ∈ L2. For the weak formulation of (1.2), only finiteness of the energy of P(u) is needed; the L2-integrability is largely an artefact of the Hilbert structure rather than a genuine requirement of the equation. This is not a limitation of [1] per se, but rather a natural consequence of the broader scope for which their framework is designed. For the specific power-law case (1.2), however, such a structure imposes unnecessary restrictions. In view of the above, we develop a new well-posedness theory for (1.2) in which the function spaces are dictated by the natural energy estimates of the equation, namely u ∈ Lm+1 and the finiteness of the energy of |u|m−1u, rather than by a pre-fixed Hilbert structure. This eliminates the unnecessary L2-regularity requirement and extends the applicability of the theory to general metric measure spaces and initial data u0 ∈ Lm+1(M, μ ). The present work constitutes a first systematic step towards the analysis of (1.2) on fractals and general metric measure spaces. Our main theorem is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.3 consists of three assertions: existence (cf. Theorem 5.9), uniqueness (cf. Theorem 5.9), and preservation of non-negativity (cf. Corollary 5.11). The existence proof relies on two classical tools: the Rothe method for time discretization and the theory of monotone operators (see, e.g., [45]). These techniques have been successfully applied to a range of variational-hemivariational inequalities [5, 44] and evolution equations [50, 56]. In all these works, the underlying functional setting is the standard Gelfand triple. In most cases, compactness of the embedding between the spaces is required; exceptions occur when the Minty trick is used to bypass compactness, as in [50]. In any case, however, the Gelfand triple structure itself remains the backbone of the argument. In our setting, however, such a Gelfand triple structure is not available: the extended Dirichlet space Fe may fail to be complete with respect to the energy norm, and no natural Hilbert space serves as the intermediate space of such a triple. Moreover, no compact embedding between the relevant function spaces is guaranteed. To overcome these di ffi culties, we introduce auxiliary spaces Vq B Lq ∩ F e and establish their uniform convexity(cf. Section 3). The construction of our weak solution is then carried out in three steps. First, for each fixed time step, we discretize the equation and obtain a family of elliptic problems, whose solvability is guaranteed by the Minty–Browder theorem. Second, we use their solutions to construct piecewise constant approximate solutions and derive uniform a priori estimates independent of the step size. Third, we pass to the limit as the step size tends to zero; the Minty trick identifies the weak limit of a subsequence of these approximate solutions as a weak solution of (1.2). To our knowledge, this is the first adaptation of the Rothe method to the signed porous medium equation on general metric measure spaces that completely bypasses the Gelfand triple framework. Uniqueness follows from a standard strict monotonicity argument applied to the weak formula-tion. The preservation of non-negativity is obtained by the following comparison principle.

μ-a.e. for almost all t ∈ (0 , T ).

We work under the standard assumptions stated at the outset of this section: local compactness, separability, and a full-support Radon measure. These ensure that the extended Dirichlet space Fe is well-defined (see [21, p. 40, the discussion preceding Theorem 1.5.2]). No additional geometric conditions are imposed.
