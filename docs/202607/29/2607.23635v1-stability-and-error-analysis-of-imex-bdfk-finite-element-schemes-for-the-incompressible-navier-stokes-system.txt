STABILITY AND ERROR ANALYSIS OF IMEX-BDF 𝑘 FINITE ELEMENT SCHEMES FOR THE INCOMPRESSIBLE NAVIER-STOKES SYSTEM 

QIANQIAN DING, YIFAN LUO, AND SHIPENG MAO 

Abstract. In this paper, we propose and analyze a class of high-order numerical schemes within a fully discrete finite element framework for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with no-slip boundary conditions. The temporal discretization employs a 𝑘 th-order ( 𝑘 = 1, · · · , 6) implicit-explicit backward difference formula (IMEX-BDF 𝑘 ), in which the nonlinear convection term is treated explicitly and the linear Stokes part implicitly, whereas the spatial discretization utilizes Taylor-Hood finite elements. We establish the stability and uniform boundedness of the numerical solution. We further establish optimal order error estimates in both space and time without any CFL-type condition, in the sense that the time step is independent of the spatial mesh size. In three dimensions, these include 𝐿 2- and 𝐻 1-norm error estimates for the velocity and 𝐿 2-norm error estimates for the pressure, with temporal convergence rates up to sixth order for all variables. Numerical experiments are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the scheme and to confirm the theoretical convergence rates. 

1. Introduction 

This work develops and analyzes a class of high-order fully discrete finite element methods for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations based on 𝑘 th-order backward dif-ferentiation formula (BDF 𝑘 ), with temporal orders 𝑘 = 1, · · · , 6. The governing equations are posed on a three-dimensional domain Ω ⊂ R3 and read

Implicit-explicit (IMEX) first-order schemes have also attracted attention [18, 20, 30], as they treat the nonlinear terms explicitly while retaining an implicit treatment of the stiff lin-ear components, thereby avoiding the solution of nonlinear systems at each time step and reducing computational costs. Second-order methods, including the Crank-Nicolson scheme and the BDF2 method, provide an improved balance between accuracy and stabil-ity and are among the most commonly employed approaches in finite element frameworks [7, 11, 12, 19, 26, 27, 34, 37]. However, even these first- and second-order schemes may be insufficient for capturing complex transient phenomena, especially in problems involving multi-scale dynamics such as turbulence and interfacial instabilities. Motivated by these limitations, the development of high-order temporal discretization techniques, such as third-, fourth-, and higher-order BDF and linear multistep methods, has become an important research direction. Compared with low-order schemes, high-order methods can significantly improve temporal accuracy under the same spatial discretization, leading to faster convergence toward the exact solution. This advantage is particularly pronounced in problems that involve fine-scale physical features, including small-scale turbulence, interfacial instabilities, and vortex dynamics. When a prescribed accuracy is targeted, high-order schemes permit larger time steps or coarser spatial meshes, thereby reducing the overall computational cost and substantially enhancing efficiency, a crucial benefit in large-scale three-dimensional simulations [15, 16]. In recent years, several key advances have been achieved in the analysis of high-order temporal discretization methods for the Navier-Stokes equations. In [9], uniform a priori bounds were established for semi-discrete high-order IMEX schemes of up to fourth-order in two-dimensional (2D) periodic domains. Later, [25] proposed a unified analytical framework for IMEX-BDF 𝑘 schemes coupled with Fourier-Galerkin spectral methods un-der periodic boundary conditions, proving 𝑘 th-order convergence for 1 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 5. This framework was further extended in [28] through the introduction of discrete orthogonal convolution kernels, yielding optimal 𝐿 2-error estimates for the velocity under condition 

𝜏 ≤ 𝑐𝑁 −2/𝑘 . It should be noted that all these works rely on periodic boundary conditions or spectral methods, which limits their applicability to realistic problems involving complex geometries and essential physical constraints. However, many practical flow phenomena occur in domains with curved boundaries, corners, or complex geometries where spectral or Fourier methods are not readily appli-cable. Finite element methods, on the other hand, offer great flexibility in handling such irregular domains and naturally accommodate a variety of boundary conditions, including the no-slip condition. Although the finite element method (FEM) is widely acknowledged as a highly effective tool for solving the Navier-Stokes equations in such complex settings, rigorous numerical analysis of high-order temporal discretization schemes within the FEM framework remains scarce. To our knowledge, the first error analysis of a third-order BDF scheme for the Navier-Stokes equations was carried out by Baker et al. [6] using a non-standard Galerkin approach, in which optimal 𝐿 2 convergence rates for the velocity were proved under the restriction 𝜏 ≤ 𝑐ℎ 2/3. Later, [1] analyzed a discontinuous Galerkin time discretization combined with continuous FEM for transient Stokes problems, deriving error estimates by exploiting the commutativity of temporal and spatial interpolation operators. For Stokes equations posed on time-dependent domains, an arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian formulation was proposed in [32], which employed BDF 𝑘 (1 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 5) for temporal dis-cretization and Taylor-Hood finite elements in space. A notable breakthrough was recently achieved by [13], who presented the first rigorous finite element error analysis for fourth-OPTIMAL ERROR ANALYSIS FOR HIGH-ORDER NAVIER-STOKES EQUATIONS 3

and fifth-order BDF discretizations of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. They established optimal error estimates for fully discrete BDF 𝑘 (𝑘 ≤ 5) finite element schemes under a CFL-type restriction of the form 𝜏 ≤ 𝐶ℎ 𝛼 , employing a fully implicit treatment of the nonlinear convection term. More recently, [35] proposed a semi-implicit finite ele-ment method based on multi-step BDF discretizations of orders up to five for the unsteady Navier-Stokes-Darcy system. Their analysis established optimal 𝐿 2-error estimates for the coupled Stokes-Darcy Ritz projection without requiring the standard 𝐻 2-regularity assump-tion. Nevertheless, the convergence analysis still relies on a CFL-type time-step restriction. It is well known that BDF methods of order greater than six are no longer zero-stable (see Chapter III of [15]), making BDF6 the highest-order member of the BDF family suitable for practical computations. The primary challenge for BDF6 stems from the absence of a positive multiplier in the sense of Nevanlinna and Odeh [33], which distinguishes it from its lower-order counterparts ( 𝑘 ≤ 5). A significant breakthrough was recently made by [3], who provided the stability analysis of BDF6 for parabolic problems. This result was further extended to the Stokes problem in [10], where up to sixth-order convergence was established. Despite these important developments, rigorous analyses for high-order finite element approximations of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations remain far from complete. In particular, all existing analyses of fourth- and fifth-order BDF finite ele-ment schemes require CFL-type restrictions, while no rigorous unconditional stability and optimal error analysis is currently available for sixth-order finite element discretizations. Motivated by the above discussion, we develop and analyze a class of fully discrete finite element approximations of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations based on BDF 𝑘 

time discretizations with orders 𝑘 = 1, · · · , 6. The nonlinear convection term is treated explicitly, while the viscous and pressure terms are discretized implicitly. This IMEX treatment of the nonlinearity yields, at each time step, a linear system with a constant coefficient matrix, thereby simplifying the computation. For BDF orders 𝑘 = 1, · · · , 5, our analysis is built upon the multiplier framework of Nevanlinna and Odeh [33]. By combining the associated discrete energy technique with finite element approximation theory, we establish unconditional stability and optimal-order error estimates for fully discrete finite element approximations of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations. The sixth-order case requires a separate and substantially more delicate analysis. Although a suitable multiplier structure for BDF6 has recently been developed for linear parabolic problems [3] and subsequently extended to the Stokes problem [10], its extension to the Navier-Stokes equations is nontrivial due to the interaction between the sixth-order multistep discretization and the explicitly treated nonlinear convection term. To address this obstacle, we recast the multistep velocity combination appearing in the extrapolated convection term as a weighted sum of sixth-order temporal derivatives and derive an identity linking the current time derivative to the solution values at the current and six previous time levels. These ingredients provide the key analytical tools for controlling the convection-induced consistency errors and recovering the full sixth-order temporal accuracy. Combining the unified analysis for 𝑘 = 1, · · · , 5 with the dedicated sixth-order analysis, we obtain unconditional energy stability and optimal error estimates for all IMEX-BDF 𝑘 schemes with 𝑘 = 1, · · · , 6. In particular, optimal convergence rates are obtained for the velocity in the 𝐿 ∞ (0, 𝑇 ; L2) and 𝐿 ∞ (0, 𝑇 ; H1) norms and for the pressure in the 𝐿 2 (0, 𝑇 ; 𝐿 2) norm. The error bounds are optimal in both space and time, yielding full 𝑘 th-order temporal convergence for every order considered. To the best of our knowledge, existing rigorous analyses of third-, fourth-, and fifth-order fully discrete BDF finite element schemes for the Navier-Stokes equations all require CFL-type restrictions. Remark 2.1. We emphasize that the present analysis differs substantially from existing results on BDF-based finite element discretizations for the Navier-Stokes equations, which are typically limited to orders 𝑘 = 3, 4, 5 and require CFL-type step-size restrictions of the form 𝜏 ≤ 𝐶ℎ 𝛼 . The unified framework developed in this work establishes unconditional stability and convergence for all orders 𝑘 = 1, · · · , 6, thereby removing any coupling condition between the time-step size and the mesh size. In particular, the unconditional stability and optimal-order error estimates for the sixth-order IMEX-BDF scheme appear to be new. A further feature of the analysis is that the nonlinear convection term is treated in an IMEX manner, so that only linear problems have to be solved at each time step. The scheme therefore retains the computational efficiency of linearized time stepping while still admitting an unconditional energy estimate without any CFL-type restriction.

In this work, we have developed and analyzed a class of fully discrete IMEX-BDF 𝑘 

finite element schemes for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, for temporal orders 

𝑘 = 1, · · · , 6. The analysis provides a unified finite element framework for BDF 𝑘 time discretizations and establishes stability as well as optimal error estimates for the fully discrete approximations. By treating the nonlinear convection term explicitly while maintaining an implicit dis-cretization for the viscous and pressure terms, the proposed formulation yields a sequence of linear problems at each time step, thereby reducing computational cost while preserving unconditional energy stability. From a theoretical perspective, the main contribution lies in the development of a unified stability and error analysis applicable to all BDF orders up to six. In particular, we prove stability and uniform boundedness of the fully discrete solutions without any restrictive condition between the time step and the mesh size. Moreover, opti-mal error estimates are derived in the three-dimensional setting, including optimal 𝐿 2- and 

𝐻 1-convergence rates for the velocity and optimal 𝐿 2-convergence rates for the pressure. The scheme achieves full 𝑘 th-order temporal accuracy for both velocity and pressure. OPTIMAL ERROR ANALYSIS FOR HIGH-ORDER NAVIER-STOKES EQUATIONS 33

Existing results for third-, fourth-, and fifth-order BDF finite element schemes typically rely on CFL-type restrictions of the form 𝜏 ≤ 𝐶ℎ 𝛼 . By contrast, the present work removes such restrictions and establishes unconditional energy stability together with optimal convergence for fully discrete IMEX-BDF finite element schemes of orders 𝑘 = 4, 5 and 6. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first rigorous unconditional stability and convergence analysis of a sixth-order IMEX-BDF finite element discretization of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. The analysis of the sixth-order scheme re-quires several new ingredients, including a suitable reformulation of the multistep structure and a discrete identity that enables the recovery of the full sixth-order temporal accuracy in the presence of explicitly treated nonlinear convection. Beyond the finite element pairs considered herein, the structure of the analysis suggests that the methodology can be extended to a broader class of inf-sup stable discretizations. In particular, it would be of considerable interest to investigate its extension to divergence-conforming 𝐻 (div ) finite element methods, such as Raviart-Thomas and Brezzi-Douglas-Marini discretizations, which provide a natural framework for exactly divergence-free velocity approximations and pressure-robust formulations. Overall, the combination of high-order temporal accuracy, unconditional stability, and computational efficiency makes the IMEX-BDF 𝑘 framework developed in this work particularly attractive for the simulation of incompressible flows with complex multiscale dynamics, especially in high-Reynolds-number regimes.
