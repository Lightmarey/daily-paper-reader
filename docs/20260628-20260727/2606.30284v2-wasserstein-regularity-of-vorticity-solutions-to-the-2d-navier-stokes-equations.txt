# Wasserstein regularity of vorticity solutions to the 2D Navier–Stokes equations 

## Viorel Barbu ∗

Abstract 

We prove herein the absolute continuity and the H¨ older-continuity in the Wasserstein metric Wp, 1 ≤ p < 2, of solutions u to 2 D Navier– Stokes vorticity equations ut − ν∆u + div( K(u)u) = 0 on (0 , ∞) × R2

with L1-initial data in the Wasserstein space Wp(R2). Herein, K is the 2D Biot–Savart operator. In this way, the vorticity flow t → u(t)can be identified with an absolutely continuous curve in the Wasser-stein space Wp(R2). One obtains also a time variation formula for the Boltzmann–Gibbs entropy of the vorticity flow. These results ex-tend to the finite Radon measure initial data and are of interest to describing the dynamic of 2D vortices and its connection with optimal transport theory. 

MSC: 60H15, 47H05, 47J05. 

Keywords: Navier–Stokes equation, vorticity, distributional solu-tion, Wasserstein space, optimal transport.

As already emphasized, the Wasserstein distances are closely related to optimal transport, processes and it is also suitable to describing instanta-neous interactions at large spatial distance in Rd. A nice illustration in this direction is the work [7] on the evolution and persistence of concentrated vortices in 2D Navier–Stokes flows. Proof of Theorem 3.2 .

As mentioned earlier, Theorem 3.2 is a direct consequence of smoothing effect of the vorticity flow t → u(t) on initial data u0 ∈ M (R2). Namely, let 

u0 ∈ P p(R2) and let u be the corresponding solution to (1.6). Let δ > 0 be ar-bitrarily small, but fixed. Moreover, by the integral representation formula (2.8) it follows as in the proof of Claim 1 (see (4.1)–(4.2)) that u(t) ∈ P p(R2) for all t ≥ 0 and, therefore, u(δ) ∈ P p(R2).
