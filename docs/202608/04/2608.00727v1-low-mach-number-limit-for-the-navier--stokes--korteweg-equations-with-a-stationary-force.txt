LOW MACH NUMBER LIMIT FOR THE NAVIER–STOKES–KORTEWEG EQUATIONS WITH A STATIONARY FORCE 

JINKAI NI ∗, LUQI WANG, YICHI ZHANG, AND ZHIPENG ZHANG 

Abstract. In this paper, we investigate the low Mach number limit for the three-dimensional compressible Navier–Stokes–Korteweg equations in the whole space under a small station-ary external force. We first construct a family of small stationary solutions uniformly with respect to the Mach number ϵ and prove that both the stationary density fluctuation and the compressible component of the stationary velocity are of order ϵ2. For ill-prepared non-stationary perturbations around these stationary solutions, we establish the existence of unique global strong solutions by combining uniform high-order energy estimates with a low-frequency Besov estimate and a Kawashima-type compensating functional. The main difficulty is that the Korteweg term not only changes the elliptic structure of the stationary problem but also modifies the dispersive mechanism of the acoustic modes. In Korteweg-symmetric variables, the associated spectral projections are uniformly bounded zero-order Fourier multipliers, while the acoustic–capillary phase is wave-like at low frequencies and Schr¨ odinger-like at high frequencies. Since the source terms generated by the stationary co-efficients are not generally integrable in time, we decompose the Duhamel source according to its time-integrability and frequency behavior. Dyadic dispersive estimates, high-frequency damping estimates, and maximal regularity for the heat equation yield the global-in-time convergence rate ϵmin {1/r, 1/2−1/p } in the mixed Besov norms Lr (0 , ∞; ˙Bsp, 1). As a conse-quence, Besov embeddings also yield quantitative convergence in the mixed Lebesgue norms 

Lr (0 , ∞; Lp).

The Korteweg model is a classical diffuse-interface description of capillary compressible fluids. Its origin goes back to Korteweg’s constitutive law for fluids with density-gradient forces [36]. A thermodynamically consistent continuum formulation was subsequently devel-oped by Dunn and Serrin [19]; see also the diffuse-interface perspective of Anderson, McFadden and Wheeler [2] and the relation with other compressible capillary models discussed by Bresch, Desjardins and Lin [5]. For a constant capillarity coefficient, the divergence of the Korteweg stress reduces, up to the pressure-gradient convention, to the third-order force κρ ∇∆ρ appear-ing in (1.1). Besides its physical role in the description of liquid–vapor interfaces and phase transitions, this term changes the analytic structure of the compressible modes by adding one derivative to the density energy and a genuinely dispersive high-frequency component. The well-posedness theory for Korteweg-type systems has developed along several comple-mentary directions. The pioneering works of Hattori and Li [23,24] established global smooth solutions near equilibrium in two and higher space dimensions. Danchin and Desjardins [13] introduced a critical-space approach for compressible Korteweg fluids, while Kotschote [37] developed a maximal-regularity theory for strong solutions. In critical and near-critical Besov frameworks, global well-posedness, nonlocal-to-local capillarity limits, and large-data local theory were studied in [6, 8, 10]; Gevrey regularity and decay were obtained in [7]. The long-time behavior generated by the capillary dissipation has been investigated by Tan and Zhang [44] and by Kawashima, Shibata and Xu [31]. Results in the presence of external forces or nontrivial reference states include the global existence and optimal decay obtained by Li [39], the potential-force analysis of Wang and Wang [48], the nonlinear stability of stationary solutions to the full NSK system by Chen and Zhao [9], and the construction and stability of time-periodic NSK solutions by Tsuda [45]. For the inhomogeneous incompress-ible NSK equations around the Rayleigh–Taylor equilibria, Jiang, Li and the fourth author of this paper [27] established in two dimensions a capillarity threshold separating nonlinear algebraic stability from instability, while Jiang, Zhang, and the fourth author of this pa-per [28] subsequently proved in three dimensions that sufficiently strong capillarity inhibits the Rayleigh–Taylor instability and yields algebraic decay of both the velocity and the density perturbation. These works show that the Korteweg tensor supplies additional density regu-larity, but they also reveal that stationary coefficients and low frequencies require a treatment different from that used around a constant equilibrium. The incompressible, or low Mach number, limit is one of the fundamental singular limits in compressible fluid dynamics. For smooth solutions, the general theory was initiated by Klainerman and Majda [33, 34]. Dispersive mechanisms for ill-prepared acoustic waves were developed by Ukai [46] and Isozaki [26]. In the viscous setting, important contributions include the zero-Mach analysis of Hoff [25], the whole-space result of Desjardins and Grenier [18], and the compactness method of Lions and Masmoudi [41] for finite-energy weak solutions. Danchin [12] established global convergence for ill-prepared data in scaling-critical spaces; related developments for full systems, singular thermodynamic limits, Lp-critical data, large solutions, and refined convergence estimates can be found in [1, 14, 15, 20, 21]. The common feature of these works is that the acoustic component must be separated from the solenoidal component and controlled by a combination of parabolic smoothing and dispersive estimates. For capillary fluids, the singular limit has additional structure. Li and Yong [40] proved a zero-Mach convergence result for the three-dimensional NSK system in the smooth regime. Antonelli, Hientzsch and Marcati [3] treated the corresponding quantum Navier–Stokes limit. More recently, Watanabe [47] obtained global large solutions and an incompressible limit for a capillary compressible Navier–Stokes system, and Fujii and Li [22] established a two-dimensional low Mach limit for large solutions in critical Fourier–Besov spaces. Those results concern perturbations of constant states, or settings without general stationary forcing. The present problem combines the capillary singular limit with perturbations around a nontrivial stationary flow in the three-dimensional whole space. Stationary forcing introduces a second and essentially independent difficulty. For the com-pressible Navier–Stokes equations, the existence and nonlinear stability of stationary flows were studied by Shibata and Tanaka [42, 43]. In scaling-invariant or weak Besov spaces, stationary incompressible flows and their stability were considered by Kaneko, Kozono and Shimizu [29], Cunanan, Okabe and Tsutsui [11], and Kozono and Shimizu [38]. For compress-ible flows, Deguchi established stability and sharp decay around stationary solutions in [16], and subsequently proved the low Mach number limit with a stationary force for the com-pressible Navier–Stokes equations in [17]. A central point in those works is that a stationary velocity in three dimensions may have a spatial tail of order |x|−1, in agreement with the far-field behavior of steady Navier–Stokes flows [35]. Such a profile is naturally accommodated by ˙B1/22,∞ but not, in general, by the stronger space ˙B1/22,1 . This motivates the mixed weak-Besov/high-Sobolev framework used below; standard facts concerning the Littlewood–Paley analysis of these spaces may be found in [4]. Deguchi [17] recently established the global-in-time low Mach number limit for the com-pressible Navier–Stokes equations with a small stationary force and ill-prepared initial data in the whole space R3. In the non-capillary problem, the stationary density fluctuation is of order O(ϵ2) only at the weak low-frequency level, whereas its full high-order estimate is of order O(ϵ); moreover, both the compressible velocity component and the incompressible projection error are generally of order O(ϵ). The Korteweg structure leads to a different sta-tionary asymptotic regime: the elliptic operator p′(ρ∞) − κρ ∞∆ provides additional density regularity and yields second-order convergence simultaneously for the stationary density and both velocity errors in the low- and high-frequency norms considered here. The distinction is also structural in the nonstationary problem. The capillary term cannot be treated as a lower-order perturbation of the non-capillary acoustic dynamics: after the ill-prepared rescal-ing, the acoustic–capillary phase is wave-like at low frequencies and Schr¨ odinger-like at high frequencies. A Korteweg-symmetric formulation is therefore required to obtain spectral pro-jections that are uniformly bounded with respect to ϵ. In addition, the linear terms generated by the stationary solution are not generally integrable in time and cannot all be included in a standard L1

To overcome these difficulties, we combine uniform energy estimates, weak low-frequency Besov control, dyadic acoustic–capillary dispersive estimates, and a decomposition of the Duhamel source into time-integrable and stationary-coefficient components.
