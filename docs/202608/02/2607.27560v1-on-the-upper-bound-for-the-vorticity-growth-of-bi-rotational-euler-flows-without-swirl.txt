ON THE UPPER BOUND FOR THE VORTICITY GROWTH OF BI-ROTATIONAL EULER FLOWS WITHOUT SWIRL 

KHAKIM EGAMBERGANOV AND DEOKWOO LIM 

Abstract. For d ≥ 4, we consider incompressible Euler flows in Rd with bi-rotational symmetry and without swirl. Our first result gives the local wellposedness of the Yudovich-type solution. The second result provides global wellposedness up to d ≤ 6. In particular, it shows that the rate of growth of the vorticity maximum coincides with the rate from axisymmetric flows without swirl, which was obtained in the paper by the second author and Jeong (Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 249(3):32, 2025) and Shao–Wei–Zhang (Acta Math. Sin. (Engl. Ser.), 42(3):663-679, 2026).

Axisymmetric solutions in 3D. Bi-rotational solutions were motivated by axisymmetric solutions of 3D Euler equations. In R3, under the cylindrical coordinates ( r, θ, z ), axisymmetric solutions without swirl are defined as 

u = ur(r, z )er + uz (r, z )ez ,

where the vorticity becomes 

ω = ωθ(r, z )eθ, ωθ(r, z ) = ∂ruz − ∂z ur,

and the Euler equations reduce to 

∂t

The global wellposedness of the solution for this equation is well-known from various works ([26, 23, 25, 8, 1]). For such globally wellposed solutions, a natural question one can ask is whether the vorticity can grow as time t goes to infinity. As the term ωθ/r is conserved along the flow, the growth of vorticity occurs when the radial distance of fluid particles from the rotation axis increases. The simplest model where such growth can be observed is the anti-parallel vortex rings ,which satisfy the following conditions: (1.10) ωθ(r, −z) = −ωθ(r, z ), ωθ(r, z ) ≤ 0, r, z ≥ 0.

This model was considered by Childress, Gilbert, and Valiant ([3, 4, 5]). There are several works regarding the upper bound and the lower bound of the growth of the vorticity maximum ∥ωθ∥L∞ . For the upper bound, the exponential growth results can be found in Majda–Bertozzi [20] and Danchin [8]. The t2-upper bound was established by Childress [3], and this can be derived by using an estimate for the velocity maximum from Feng–Sverak [11]. Recently, the t4/3-upper bound was obtained by Jeong and the author [18] for compactly supported initial vorticity with ωθ

In addition, for single-signed vorticity, Maffei–Marchioro [19] provides t1/4 ln( e + t)-upper bound using the conservation of the fluid impulse ∥rω θ∥L1 . For lower bounds, Choi–Jeong established a patch-type solution satisfying (1.10) and t1/15 −-lower bound. This was enhanced by Gustafson–Miller–Tsai [12] obtaining t3/8−-lower bound. High dimension. There are several works which analyzed axisymmetric Euler equations in high dimensions d ≥ 3 ([7, 17, 12]). Although such cases are not physical, there are several works suggesting that there is a good chance that finite-time singularity formation of a solution with some smooth initial data can occur when the dimension is large ([9, 22, 13, 14]). w0/r 2 ∈ L2,1(R4). The bi-rotational equation can be considered as a special case of the lake equation with the depth function d(r, s ) = rnsm for any n, m ≥ 1. This appears in the work of Khesin–Yang [16] and Yang [27]. Previously, Choi, Jeong, and the author [6] proved the global wellposedness of the bi-rotational solutions with the Yudovich-type regularity and decay near the axes r = 0, s = 0, and at infinity for the 4D case n = m = 1.
