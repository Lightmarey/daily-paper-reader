# Solvability of the Poisson-Dirichlet problem in domains with boundaries of mixed dimension 

Léo Mandô ∗

Abstract 

We extend several characterizations of the Lp-solvability of the homogeneous Dirichlet prob-lem to (possibly degenerate) elliptic operators L = −div (wA ∇) defined on a large class of open sets Ω in Rn. This framework encompasses not only uniformly elliptic operators in Lips-chitz domains, but also Caffarelli-Sylvestre-type operators, and boundaries ∂Ω that are not 

(n − 1) -dimensional, for instance. We prove that, for 1 < p < ∞, the Lp-solvability of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem is equivalent to the solvability of the Poisson-Dirichlet problem 

Lu = wf −div (wF ), i.e. to the existence of a solution u satisfying a Lp non-tangential estimate whenever f and F belong to suitable weighted Lp tent spaces. Furthermore, it is also equivalent to the solvability of the Poisson-Regularity problem for data in appropriate weighted Lp′

tent spaces, where estimates are obtained for ∇u rather than u.

Keywords: Degenerate elliptic operators, Boundary value problems, Dirichlet problem, Poisson-Dirichlet problem, tent spaces.

# 1 Introduction 

1.1 Background 

In recent decades, the study of the (homogeneous) Dirichlet problem 

(

Lu = 0 in Ω

u = g on ∂Ω, (1.1) where Ω is an open set of Rn and L = − div( A∇) is a uniformly elliptic operator in divergence form, has been an area of extensive research in harmonic analysis, pushing towards more general conditions: non-smooth coefficients A, rough boundaries ∂Ω, non-continuous boundary data g. Our interest in this paper will lie in the solvability of the Dirichlet problem with Lp data; that is to say, to find solutions of (1.1) that satisfy the estimate 

∥N u∥Lp(∂Ω) ≤ C∥g∥Lp(∂Ω) , (1.2) where N u is the non-tangential maximal function of u, see (1.19) below for the definition. When L is the Laplacian and Ω is a Lipschitz domain, Dahlberg showed in [Dah77] that the harmonic measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the surface measure, and that the corresponding Radon derivative (called the Poisson kernel) belongs to the reverse Hölder class B2.Putting this in our context, it means that in Lipschitz domains, the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian is L2-solvable. Even 50 years after Dahlberg’s paper, we do not know what is the geometric characterization of domains that ensures the L2-solvability of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian. What we know is that in Chord-Arc Domains (CAD) - a natural extension of Lipschitz domains - the harmonic measure is A∞-absolutely continuous 1 with respect to the surface measure, which translates to the fact that the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian is solvable only in Lp for a large p ∈ (1 , ∞)

depending of the CAD constants; indeed, the A∞-absolute continuity for CAD was tackled by David and Jerison in [DJ90], while in [Jer83] Jerison provided, for each p ∈ (1 , ∞), a CAD where the Lp-solvability of the Dirichlet problem fails. Chord Arc-Domains appears to be almost optimal for the existence of a p ∈ (1 , ∞) that gives the Lp solvability of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian: assuming boundaries of Hausdorff dimension n − 1 and ample access to the boundary (all in a quantitative manner), the converse was studied in [Hof+17], [Azz21] and [Azz+20]. Beyond the Laplacian, the Lp-solvability of the Dirichlet problem was widely studied. Early on, Caffarelli, Fabes, and Kenig in [CFK81], and by Modica and Mortola in [MM80] exhibited some uniformly elliptic operators on smooth domains for which the Lp-solvability of the Dirichlet problem fails for every 1 < p < ∞. Positive results were subsequently obtained under additional assumptions on the coefficients: for symmetric coefficients in Lipschitz domains in [JK81a], for 

t-independent operators in Rn

For DKP operators, the equivalence between CAD and the existence of some p ∈ (1 , ∞) that ensures the Lp-solvability of the Dirichlet was achieved in [Hof+21]. 1.2 General setting 

We consider an open set Ω ⊂ Rn, with n ≥ 2, a Borel measure m on Rn, and a Borel measure μ

supported on ∂Ω (both non-negative, non identically zero and locally finite). Our geometric setting is based on the one found in [DFM23], although we do not assume the Harnack Chain condition like [DFM23] does, and instead we consider a measure m on the whole space Rn (instead of a measure supported in Ω) satisfying a Poincaré-Sobolev inequality on every ball centered in ¯Ω.Since our hypotheses on Ω are weaker than the ones from [DFM23], our settings encompasses a large variety of domains and operators. The classical case, i.e. domains with (n − 1) -Ahlfors regular boundaries and uniformly elliptic operators, is of course included. Let us recall for the sake of completeness that a set E ⊂ Rn is said to be d-Ahlfors regular when there exists C > 0 such that 

C−1rd ≤ H d(E ∩ B(x, r )) ≤ Cr d for x ∈ E, 0 < r < diam( E), (1.8) where Hd is the d-dimensional Hausdorff measure. If Ω = Rn \ E is the complement of a d-Ahlfors regular set E of dimension d ≤ n − 2, functions in W 1,2(Ω) do not have a trace, and one must consider degenerate operators like L = − div( δd−(n−1) ∇), with δ(x) := dist (x, ∂ Ω) , to be able to study the Lp-Dirichlet problem (see [DFM19], [MZ19], [Fen22] or [DM23] for examples of the study of the A∞-absolute continuity of the elliptic measure and the Lp solvability of the Dirichlet problem in such settings). For an explanation of how our hypotheses encompasses the aforementioned cases, as well as others examples where our assumptions hold, see Chapter 3 in [DFM23].
