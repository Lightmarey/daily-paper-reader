UNIFIED THEORY FOR REGULARITY PERSISTENCE OF VORTEX PATCH BOUNDARIES 

MARC MAGA ˜NA 

Abstract. We establish a unified local theory for the persistence of Sobolev regularity of vortex patch boundaries in a family of two-dimensional active scalar equations with radial convolution kernels K(|x − y|). The class includes the 2D Euler equation, the generalized SQG equation in the locally integrable range 0 < β < 1, and the quasi-geostrophic shallow water equation. Under natural assumptions on K (smoothness, integrability near the origin, monotonicity, and polynomial growth), we prove that if the initial boundary belongs to H3(T)and satisfies the arc-chord condition, then the contour dynamics equation admits a unique local solution in C([0 , T ]; H3(T)). Under a stronger integrability condition on the kernel, we also obtain local existence of H2 solutions. The proof combines Sobolev energy estimates for the contour equation with quantitative control of the arc-chord quantity.

As in the Euler case, the transport structure of (1) guarantees that if the initial datum is a characteristic function, the solution remains a characteristic function of some evolving domain for all times. A fundamental question in the study of vortex patches is whether the initial regularity of the boundary persists over time. More precisely, if the boundary of the initial domain ∂Ω0 belongs to a given regularity class (for instance, a H¨ older space Ck,σ or a Sobolev space W s,p ), one seeks to determine whether ∂Ω( t) remains in the same class for all times, or at least locally in time. For the 2D Euler equation in the plane, this problem was raised in the 1980s and resolved in the H¨ older setting by Chemin [7], who proved global persistence of C1,σ regularity (0 < σ < 1) using paradifferential calculus. Shortly thereafter, Bertozzi and Constantin [2] obtained an alternative proof based on geometric methods in classical analysis. Related propagation results for singular vortex patches were later obtained, for instance, in [11]. other domains, such as bounded domains and the half-plane [12, 21]. On the other hand, it has recently been shown by Kiselev and Luo that the Euler vortex patch problem is ill-posed in C2

[22], highlighting the delicate nature of the regularity threshold. For the active scalar models introduced above, the persistence of boundary regularity has also been extensively studied. In the case of the SQG equation ( β = 1), Rodrigo [31] proved local existence and uniqueness of C∞ patches via a Nash–Moser implicit function theorem. For the generalized SQG equations (0 < β < 2), local-in-time persistence of Sobolev regularity for the patch boundary is by now well understood. Gancedo [14] established local existence and uniqueness for patch boundaries parametrized in Hk(T), k ≥ 3, for 0 < β < 1, and existence in the same class for β = 1. Uniqueness in the latter case was later proved by C´ ordoba, C´ ordoba, and Gancedo [9]. These results were subsequently extended by Chae, Constantin, C´ ordoba, Gancedo, and Wu [6] to the full range 0 < β < 2, with k ≥ 4. More recently, Gancedo and Patel [16] obtained local existence for patch boundaries in H2(T)for 0 < β < 1 and in H3(T) for 1 < β < 2. In the critical case β = 1, Gancedo, Nguyen, and Patel [15] proved local well-posedness in H2+ s for arbitrarily small s > 0. Several ill-posedness results are known for these models. Kiselev and Luo [24] proved strong ill-posedness of C2,σ and W 2,p (p̸ = 2) patch solutions for gSQG in the range 0 < β < 1. The formation of splash singularities, that is, configurations where the boundary self-intersects at a point while remaining otherwise smooth, has been ruled out for the full range of β in [17, 23, 20]. In the half-plane with a rigid boundary, finite-time singularity formation has been established for 0 < β < 13 (see [25, 26, 16]; see also [38] for further improvements). For the quasi-geostrophic shallow water (QGSW) equation, Yudovich theory and global well-posedness of vortex patches in C1,σ was recently established in [28]. More generally, for the 2D loglog-Euler type equation, Tan, Xue, and Xue [33] revisited Elgindi’s result [13] and proved global propagation of patch boundary regularity with a loss of H¨ older exponent, namely from 

C1,σ to C1,σ −ε. They also pointed out that, under additional assumptions on the multiplier, this loss can be avoided. In the complementary regime, where the Osgood condition fails, Miao, Tan, Xue, and Xue [30] showed that finite-time singularities may occur for patch solutions in the half-plane. Finally, persistence of boundary regularity has also been studied for a broader class of nonlinear transport equations in which the velocity field is given directly by convolution, v = K ∗ω, without imposing the divergence-free condition. This includes, for instance, the aggregation equation in 

Rn with Newtonian kernel, K = ∇N , studied in [4, 3]. Another important contribution is due to Cantero, Mateu, Orobitg, and Verdera [5], who consider odd kernels in Rn, homogeneous of degree −(n − 1) and smooth away from the origin, and prove global-in-time existence and uniqueness of patch solutions with C1,σ boundary, together with persistence of this regularity. Their uniqueness result is formulated within the class of characteristic functions of C1,σ domains, rather than as a general Yudovich-type theory for arbitrary bounded compactly supported data. Their framework allows for velocity fields with nontrivial divergence and includes, among other examples, kernels of the form L(∇N ) where L is a linear mapping from Rn into itself, such as the 3D quasi-geostrophic equation and the Cauchy transport equation in the plane. The planar case was later revisited by Verdera [35], where the same global patch regularity result is obtained by different methods. For the Cauchy kernel, a particular case of the class in [5], Clop and Sengupta [8] proved existence of solutions for bounded, compactly supported initial data, even though the associated velocity field may have unbounded divergence, using compactness 4 MARC MAGA ˜NA 

properties of quasiconformal mappings. In this setting, the main difficulty is that the velocity field may have non-zero, and in some cases unbounded, divergence. In this paper, we propose a unified framework encompassing a broad class of kernels K (including the examples above), and establish local-in-time persistence of Sobolev regularity for the patch boundary under explicit and verifiable assumptions on K. Our approach is inspired by the work of Hmidi, Xue, and Xue [19], who developed a unified theory for V-state structures in active scalar equations. Remark. The assumptions on the kernel have been stated in a form that keeps the energy estimates as transparent as possible. The smoothness assumption (A1) can be relaxed: it suffices that K ∈ C3(R \ { 0}) for Theorem 1.1, and K ∈ C2(R \ { 0}) for Theorem 1.2. In a similar way, the global monotonicity assumption in (A4) is used mainly to avoid repeated decompositions between small and large scales. One expects the same arguments to work under a weaker condition in which, for the relevant derivatives, |K(n)| is required to be decreasing only near the origin, while away from the origin the derivatives satisfy polynomial growth bounds analogous to (A3). Indeed, the singular part of the estimates only uses monotonicity at small scales, whereas the contribution from the region |γ(x)−γ(x−x′)| ≳ 1 can be controlled by Sobolev bounds on the curve and polynomial growth of the kernel. We keep the stronger formulation in order to avoid additional technical decompositions. The analytical framework developed in this paper follows closely the approach introduced by Gancedo in [14] and further developed in [16]. The argument is based on the contour-dynamics energy method introduced in the aforementioned works, but it is implemented here at the level of a general radial kernel K. This requires isolating the precise kernel properties needed in the estimates and replacing the explicit computations available for the gSQG kernels by structural bounds valid for the whole class considered. Appendix A. Further examples of admissible kernels

We first recall a useful criterion based on complete monotonicity, which makes the monotonicity assumptions on the derivatives essentially automatic.
