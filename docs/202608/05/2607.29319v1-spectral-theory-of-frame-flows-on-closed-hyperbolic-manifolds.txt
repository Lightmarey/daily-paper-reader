SPECTRAL THEORY OF FRAME FLOWS ON CLOSED HYPERBOLIC MANIFOLDS 

JULIEN NAMAZI 

Abstract. We prove a resolvent estimate for the generator of the frame flow on hyperbolic manifolds away from vertical lines of resonances. A byproduct of the proof is an optimal essential spectral gap property for the generator, hence giving another proof of exponential mixing of frame flows with respect to the volume measure of the frame bundle. This extends the result of [DGH21] in dimension 3 to any dimension. We make an extensive use of the Borel-Weil calculus developed in [CL24] to overcome difficulties of this higher-dimensional case.

Of particular importance is the case of the geodesic flow on the unit tangent bundle of a manifold with a negative sectional curvature everywhere [Ano67]. The Anosov property is of central importance in the realm of dynamical systems: the central direction RX,not present for Anosov diffeomorphisms, makes the study of their speed of mixing very challenging. Definition 1.1. An Anosov flow generated by a vector field X is said to be contact if there exists a smooth 1-form α, defined by α(X) = 1 and ker( α) = Eu ⊕ Es, satisfying that dα |Eu⊕Es is non-degenerate. A celebrated result is the exponential mixing of contact Anosov flows with respect to the Liouville form α ∧ (dα )n−1 (if dim( M ) = 2 n − 1) [Dol98, Liv04, NZ14, FT24]. In particular, the geodesic flow is exponentially mixing on negatively curved manifolds with respect to the Liouville measure on their unit tangent bundles. The more recent approach in [NZ14, FT24] uses tools from microlocal and semiclassical analysis, whereas the one in [Dol98, Liv04] is more dynamically oriented and use of Markov partitions [Dol98] or Lasota-Yorke inequalities [Liv04]. In this paper, we will adopt the more recent point of view involving microlocal analysis. The key ingredient to obtain the exponential mixing of (φt) is to obtain resolvent estimates for the unbounded operator X on the anisotropic Sobolev space Hs(SM ),a functional space adapted to the hyperbolic behaviour of the flow. Together with the spectral gap property, one obtains the exponential decay of correlations. In the context of smooth r-normally hyperbolic trapping, the work [Dya16] shows spectral gap and resolvent estimate properties for operators whose symbol has a Hamiltonian flow presenting a structure similar to the Anosov case. In fact, the ideas in [Dya16] apply to treat exponential mixing for contact Anosov flows such that their stable and unstable bundles are smooth. However the smoothness assumption is known to be very rigid, and only occur for Anosov flows built from the geodesic flow on hyperbolic manifold [HK90, BFL90]. In the recent paper [CG21], the authors were able to prove exponential mixing for contact Anosov flow in dimension 3. In this case Eu,s are known to be C1,α regular, which allows after some work to use the semiclassical proof of [Dya16]. The present work will assume smoothness of Eu,s , but the flows under consideration will present additional(s) neutral directions: this is the setting of partially hyperbolic dynamics .

In this paper, we will consider a specific case of isometric extensions: the frame flow over (M n, g ). Write SM := {(x, v ) ∈ T M | gx(v, v ) = 1 } and (φt) the geodesic flow on 

SM . On the orthonormal frame bundle F M → SM , we may define the frame flow (Φ t).It corresponds to the parallel transport of a frame (x, v, e1, . . . , en−1) along the geodesic starting at (x, v ), see Section §3.1 for precise definitions. This flow is an SO (n − 1) 

extension of the geodesic flow on SM . We write XF M the generator of this flow. Now assume that (M, g ) is closed and has everywhere negative sectional curvature. Thanks to the fact that it satisfies Definition 1.2 with G = SO (n−1) , the frame flow is partially hyperbolic. We have the splitting 

T (F M ) = EuF M ⊕ EsF M ⊕ RXF M ⊕ VF M ,(1.4) where the vertical bundle VF M is tangent to the fiber direction of F M → SM and is invariant by dΦt. The bundles Eu,s F M are also invariant subbundles additionally satisfying the usual contraction and expansion properties 

∀p ∈ F M, ∀v ∈ EsF M , ∥dxΦt(v)∥φt(p) ≤ Ce −νt ∥v∥x, if t > 0,(1.5) 

∀p ∈ F M, ∀v ∈ EuF M , ∥dxΦt(v)∥φt(x) ≤ Ce νt ∥v∥x, if t < 0.

Since the work [BG80], much effort has been made to understand the dynamical property of this flow under the negative curvature assumption on M , see [CLMS24a, CLMS24b]. For instance, it is known since [Moo] that it is exponentially mixing for the natural volume measure if M is an hyperbolic manifold, however the proof relies heavily on representation theory. In [DGH21], Guillarmou and Delarue were able to incorporate the microlocal toolbox from [FS11] in the setting of the frame flow on hyperbolic 3-manifolds. They were able to prove exponential mixing for the normalized volume using those techniques. Note that F M → SM is a circle bundle if n = 3 , this allows the use of a fiberwise Fourier decomposition of elements in D′(F M ), namely distributions on F M . The spectral gap property for Anosov flows then becomes a gap which is uniform in the Fourier parameter. Higher dimensions seemed more difficult to tackle due to the fact that F M → SM is in general a SO (n − 1) -principal bundle. Thanks to the recent important work [CL24], Cekić and Lefeuvre were able to construct the so-called Borel-Weil calculus . This is a (semiclassical) pseudodifferential calculus that is adapted to 

G-equivariant pseudodifferential operators on F M , XF M fits in this class. To such operator we may associate a family of operators acting on specific line bundles. We will heavily use the Borel-Weil calculus of [CL24] to tackle the case of higher-dimensional hyperbolic manifold. Different approaches using Dolgopyat’s inequality led to the proof of exponential mixing of the frame flow outside of the compact case on hyperbolic manifolds [SW21, LP23, LPS26].
